FROM CONCEPT TO EVIDENCE

One mechanism. Measurable progress.

Start with geometry and deployment timing. Earn the next feature through testing.

The first product hypothesis

Updated requirements: pre-impact sensing, layers and gap coverage ↗

A modular impact-mitigation layer for workers at height, worn alongside existing PPE. Begin with an instrumented dummy and cranial/collar, torso and pelvis modules. Leg modules remain an exploratory extension.

Expand outward, with physical limits

Inextensible inner panels and internal tethers define a fixed body-side envelope. Size-specific patterns limit expansion mechanically. Software selects modules; it does not establish a safe neck clearance. This visualization holds inner chamber faces fixed but does not prove seam behavior, fit, or clearance across movement.

Our hardest unanswered question

Can enough usable cushioning be packaged into a wearable system without unsafe neck loads, excessive mass, or deployment interference? “It inflates” is not the same as “it protects.”

What to defer

Medical diagnosis, automatic respiratory sealing, fire protection and chemical isolation each require separate evidence. No simulated emergency alerts are sent anywhere.

Candidate components

Candidate parts illustrate feasible technology classes. They are not a procurement list, safety certification, or a finalized bill of materials.

Evidence gates

  1. Fit rig: map inward intrusion, gaps and anchor displacement on a dummy through representative poses. Use an external low-pressure supply; establish test limits with a qualified lab.
  2. Single chamber: record pressure-time, volume, leakage, repeatability and force–displacement curves. Replace assumed P·A·s with measured work.
  3. Drop rig: compare matched protected / baseline instrumented trials, including partial inflation and puncture. Record high-rate acceleration and force; do not infer safety from animation.
  4. Detection: baseline thresholds on KFall, split by participant. Report missed falls, false triggers per exposure time and lead-time distributions. Then collect representative occupational motion.
  5. Integration: validate helmet/harness compatibility, neck loads, field servicing and independent review before any human deployment study.

Useful AI, later

Estimate orientation and classify motion only after a transparent baseline. Keep deployment authority local and independently testable. A phone assistant can explain logs and coordinate a user-approved response; it is not the safety controller.

Model boundaries

Gravity: 9.81 m/s². Fall timing uses a configured vertical translation to ground contact. Pedestrian motion begins with an assumed fraction of vehicle speed transferred to the dummy; the initial strike is unprotected. Vehicle deformation, hood contact, spin, joint forces and friction are not solved.

Cushion work uses constant effective gauge pressure × contact area × available stroke. It ignores transient gas flow, venting, textile mechanics, local anatomy and load redistribution. Residual energy is a model diagnostic, not an injury metric.

Timing starts from an idealized event onset. No fall detector or trained AI runs in this version. Faults illustrate total inhibition or loss of one chamber, not a validated fail-safe strategy.
